What the votes show

Five analyses that read the voting record rather than narrate it. Every number on this page is recomputed from the corpus as it stands on disk — including the Council's drafts and the vetoed votes restored in September 2026 — and every chart states its base and what it cannot carry. Hover for values, click to drill in, and open any table for the exact figures.

Analysis 1 · the veto

Who vetoed, when — and how much of the published record the corpus holds

A veto is a permanent member's vote against a draft that otherwise had the required majority; procedural votes cannot be vetoed. The corpus now reads vetoes from its voting rounds, where the vetoed drafts sit even though they never received an S/RES number. The left panel sets each member's count against the published reference; the right panel spreads the same casts over time. Click any year to list its vetoed votes below.

Analysis 2 · passing and failing

How often a text put to the Council fails

Every resolution the Council adopted, and every vote on which a text did not pass — split into vetoed and failed (short of the required majority, no veto needed). Resolutions adopted with no vote in the corpus — chiefly the 2020–21 written procedure, when the Council could not meet — are shown hatched. Switch to share to compare years with very different volumes.

Analysis 3 · the permanent five

How often each pair of permanent members votes the same way

Agreement is the share of votes on which both members cast the same direction — for, against or abstain — counted over every recorded Council vote, so the drafts that failed are in the base, not just the resolutions that passed. Pick a period to redraw the matrix; click a cell (or a pair below) to follow it across the decades. Seats changed hands twice: China's seat passed from the Republic of China to the People's Republic in October 1971, and the USSR's to the Russian Federation in December 1991.

The frame behind each bar is the session's expected output, taken from its resolution numbering; the bar is what the corpus holds. Solid bars are sessions at 80% coverage or better. Analysis 5 · where states stand

Each state's split when the Assembly takes a recorded vote

Each bar is one country's recorded votes, split into yes, abstain and no. Most Assembly business is adopted without a vote, so a roll call is by construction the contested minority — and many early ones are votes on amendments that failed, which is why "no" is not the same as "isolated". The fourth measure, voting with the majority (casting the most common direction in that vote), is the one to read for isolation. Narrow the period, change the sort, or search for a country.
